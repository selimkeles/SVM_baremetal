                                 ABSTRACT

    This project aims to design and simulation of the 3-Phase voltage source inverter
with the Space Vector Pulse Width Modulation(SVPWM) technique. For this purpose
both Conventional and Carrier Based Space Vector Modulation techniques have been
examined and 2 different designs created. 2 Level Pulse Width Modulated-Voltage
Source Inverter(PWM-VSI) topology and a three-phase RL load used for the hardware
implementation of the project. In Conventional SVPWM reference signals for the
PWM-VSI circuit have been obtained by using Clarke Transformation. For Carrier
Based SVPWM model, modified three-phase sinusoids have been created for reference
signals using real-time relations. Results and analysis of the simulation model made by
using MATLAB-Simulink software compared to the calculated values.

   Implementation of the model made by using STM32F407VGT6 microcontroller as
Digital Signal Processor. Also, the VSI circuit designed in KiCAD software and printed
for hardware implementation. The design philosophy of the Printed Circuit Board and
the algorithmic structure of the DSP are explained in detail. In the implementation, the
Conventional SVPWM method is preferred. 3-Phase induction motor used as a load for
measurements and observations. Cost analysis of the system has shared respectively.

1. INTRODUCTION

    Demanded variable frequency and variable amplitude for AC machines also discover-
ies and improvements on chemical energy storage units which stores DC power, opened
up new discovery areas in electronics for DC-AC conversion. We use the engineering
term Inverter" for these special circuits. Today, the IEEE dictionary similarly defines
Inverter (electric power) as "a machine, device, or system that changes direct-current
power to alternating-current power". Inverters divide into two sections called Volt-
age Source Inverters(VSI) and Current Source Inverters(CSI). Pulse Width Modulated
Voltage Source Inverters widely used in industry since the development of thyristors
and controllable semiconductors in the 1960s. There are many topologies for different
circumstances that have advantages and disadvantages to each other. Also, Inverters
can be fed from rectified AC sources since the grid signal has a fixed frequency and
amplitude that is decided by distributors.

   Pulse Width Modulated Voltage Source Inverters(PWM-VSI) become much more
efficient and practical to use since the invention of IGBTs. IGBTs are a special kind
of semiconductor that consists of advantages over both MOSFETs and BJTs. IGBTs
have very low internal resistance like BJTs also they don't need huge gate currents like
MOSFETs. Besides this; for the same VSI topologies, different modulation techniques
have been invented e.g. Sinusoidal PWM, Third Harmonic PWM, and Space Vector
PWM. The difference between these modulation techniques is derived from switching
pattern differences which result in differences in DC bar utilization and design com-
plexity. They have both advantages and disadvantages. For example, Sinusoidal PWM
has an easier design procedure but less DC Bus utilization compared to others, also
Space Vector Modulation has a more complex design but this complexity results in less
harmonic distortion compared to the Third Harmonic PWM [1].

                                                        1
    Space Vector Modulation contains a huge place over these methods which is also
the topic of this thesis. In 1982 conference paper by Pfaff, Weschta and Wick described
creating switching waveforms utilizing a technique for electric machine analysis known
as space vectors [2]. The basic idea is that you analyze the state of a motor or generator
by considering per-phase quantities together as a group. This idea had been around
since the early 1900s but was primarily developed by R.H. Park at General Electric,
who published a 1929 paper on the so-called "two-reaction" (d-q) theory [3]. The term
"space vector" appears to be of European origin (by two Hungarian professors, K. P.
Kovacs and I. Racz), referring to the use of symmetrical components and Park's two-
reaction theory, not just as abstract quantities used to analyze three-phase power, but
as real physical quantities representing the spatial variation of currents or magnetic
fluxes in electric motors or generators. The technique used by Pfaff, Weschta, and
Wick for generating switching waveforms is to treat 3-Phase PWM generation as a
single task. Instead of generating three independent switching waveforms, treat the
3-Phase bridge as one unit. A 1986 conference paper by Van Der Broeck, Skudelny,
and Stanke described the "optimal" use of space vector modulation in PWM to reduce
switching losses and current ripple. The Van Der Broeck paper has been cited by over
600 papers about motor control and is really the foundation for PWM in modern digital
motor control [4].

   It must be noted that Space Vector Modulation is much more a general idea and does
not need any fixed frequency PWM. But technique mentioned in Van Der Broeck's
papers uses fixed frequency and is called Conventional SVPWM. [5] When someone
refers to Space Vector Modulation usually means Van Der Broeck's method.

   This thesis contains an Inverter design with Space Vector PWM. In the Literature
Review chapter, 3-Phase PWM-VSI topology was examined. Also popular and widely
used switching methods and techniques with Space Vector PWM are reviewed in a
section under the chapter. Simulation Model and Results are shared in the Simulation
of the System chapter. Implementation of the model and a detailed explanation of the
design are shared in the Implementation of the System chapter. Measurements and
observations of the implemented model, also comparisons of simulation results can be
found in the Experimental Results chapter. Cost Analysis and Conclusion of the thesis
may found in order.

2. Literature Review on 3 Phase Voltage Source Inverters

    In this chapter, analysis is made on 2 Level 3-Phase PWM-VSI circuit since it is the
same topology that used in this thesis model and also shared by other modulation tech-
niques too. Also a couple of switching techniques that help to better understand Space
Vector PWM examined. 180° and 120° Conduction methods are widely used in the
industry throughout history for their ease of usability. Also, very common techniques
Sinusoidal PWM and slightly different 3rd Harmonic PWM mentioned respectively.
3rd Harmonic PWM's philosophy and its waveforms plays a huge role in understanding
Space Vector PWM since the development of both techniques is aimed at the same
goal.

Switch with 3 legs can be seen at this topology. We classify the leg's upper switches
as the high side and lower switches as the low side. A leg's high side and low side
switches can not be closed at the same time due to the possible shorting of DC Source.
Freewheeling Diodes are used with every IGBT to prevent switch time delays or damage
on IGBTs while turning off.

2.2 Switching techniques in 3-phase VSI circuits

    There are many switching techniques out there when the topic is 3-phase PWM-VSI
circuits. But in this chapter, widely used ones which play a role in the development
of modulation techniques are examined. Respectively 180° and 120° Conduction tech-
niques are examined first since they exist from the start of PWM-VSI topology, also
they give a fundamental idea of PWM-VSI. After that, more complex techniques like
Sinusoidal PWM and 3rd Harmonic Injection are reviewed for clarification of Space
Vector PWM.

    In 180° conduction gate signals applied as high logic state in the first half period
which closes the high side switch. And in the second half of the period low logic state
is applied which opens the high side switch. The low side switch is the complement
of the high side switch. Since High Side conducting for 180° technique is called 180°
Conduction. To obtain 3-Phase and 120° phase difference between output signals,
same control signals with phase difference -120° and 120° applied to the B and C legs.

2.3 Space Vector Pulse Width Modulation

    SVPWM (Space Vector Pulse Width Modulation) is a kind of technique where the
Inverter's switches are treated as a single unit. Gate Signals are created by using
algebraic equations. In SVPWM we consider Reference Voltages as vectors in the 2D
plane. Using an algebraic operation called Clarke Transform we obtain 2 Reference
Signals with a 90° phase difference. These two signals are AC parameters. Then in one
cycle, summation of these to vector creates full circular movement. Gate Signals are
created by using this vector's properties.

Gate Drive Circuit
    Due to topology of PWM-VSI circuit gate signals, direct connection of gate signals

to the switching semiconductors may end in catastrophic faults. Connecting reference
points of the gate signals to the emitters of the IGBT's, results with a short circuit
path between low side common ground and high side emitters through Digital Signal
Processor ground. Low side switches out of the system in this condition due to short
circuit path. In normal gating sequence this causes shorting of DC BUS through high
side switches which ends up frying semiconductors, DSP and PCB. In grid without
circuit breakers might cause fire hazard too.

   To prevent these effects gate driver integrated circuits called IS314W used for optical
isolation between DSP and IGBTs' gates. IS314W has enough insulation margins for
usage between DC Bus and DSP. IS314W also allows to operate in 10kHz bandwith
which is system's operation frequency. Gate Drive Circuit designed as suggested in
its data sheet which can be seen in figure 4.2 below. Since low side switches has
common ground in emitter, only one isolated voltage source supplied to the gate drivers
connected to them.

4.2 Software Implementation

    STM32F407VGT6 microcontroller based software designed to generate Space Vec-
tor PWM gate signals. In this section; the design processes of software and the philos-
ophy behind the design are explained in detail.

   Using an advanced timer peripheral of the DSP made 3-Phase PWM generation
uncomplicated. Complementary output feature used to generate low side gate signals.
Also, dead time module is used to prevent any overlap between high side and low
side switches. Dead time duration was selected by observing switching durations of
the IGBTs. Test results shared in figure 5.1 in Experimental Results and Discussions
section.

   A timer interrupt routine designed for 100µS gaps, which implements a 10kHz sam-
pling ratio of the system. Interrupt routine performs necessary calculations for the
open loop system at the desired frequency which is taken from the user by ADC pe-
ripheral. Using shadow registers, compare registers of the PWM output updates itself
synchronously. In every sample, calculations of the next sample's dwell times are per-
formed using the Conventional SVPWM method.

   Every cycle uses normalization arithmetic for the modulation index to adjust the
flux of the load concerning the output frequency. Frequencies greater than the bound-
aries of the modulation index results in flux weakening since the algorithm limits the
modulation index at a maximum of 1 point to prevent overmodulation.

7. Conclusion

    In conclusion Space Vector PWM requires much more tedious calculations for in-
verter drive compared to other techniques, but the result is superior to the others. DC
Bus utilization is maximum mathematically available for the linear region and also has
lower harmonic distortion in lower frequencies.

   In this project theoretical analysis was made and shared on SVPWM to be more
understandable. Simulation for system performed to obtain reference results for easier
implementation of the system. Simulation results and measurements match with the-
oretical values. Reference Signals and Gate Signals match the theoretical ones. There
are no uncharacteristic harmonics due to the correct selection of sampling period also
DC bus utilization in %57 levels. Using both Conventional and Carrier Based meth-
ods results in the same waveforms which also clarified in Simulation Model and FFT
results.

   In real-life implementation of the system, Conventional Method used since it was eas-
ier to design using Digital Signal Processor. The design process of the system is shared
in detail. The resultant waveforms completely match the simulation results. Also,
physical observations on AC motor, which is used as load, clarifies smooth operation.

   To improve and develop the project further, a closed loop system might be added to
the system. Observation of the rotor speed in continuous time makes torque control
possible. Also, that kind of system autocorrects itself without needing any operator for
various loads on the shaft.
